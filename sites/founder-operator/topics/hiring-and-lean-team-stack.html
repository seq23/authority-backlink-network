The lean team's operating stack

A lean-team operating system is the set of role definitions, work records and tools a small team uses to deliver work. It starts with outcomes and decision authority rather than software selection. Hiring scorecards, realistic work samples and documented handoffs help separate a staffing problem from unclear role design.

Quick answer

Define the work and authority first, assess candidates against the same criteria, and add tools only when a handoff or record actually needs one.

What this guide is best for: Role scorecards, work samples and onboarding, the tools a small team needs, and the SOP, resource and fundraising libraries that keep it running.

Frequently asked questions

What should a role scorecard contain?

Name the outcomes the role owns, decisions it can make, dependencies on other people and evidence that would show success. Compare those expectations with the authority and resources available. If the role cannot reasonably deliver its stated outcomes, adjust the job design before using the scorecard to judge a candidate.

How should I compare work samples?

Give candidates equivalent instructions, a stated time boundary and the same scoring criteria before work starts. Use a task that resembles the role without extracting usable unpaid client work. Review tradeoffs and reasoning alongside the artifact; a polished submission does not explain what resources or assumptions produced it.

When is a contractor different from an employee?

The distinction depends on the working relationship and applicable rules, not merely the label in a brief. The IRS publishes federal classification guidance; other obligations may require qualified advice. Use that source to frame questions before comparing a contractor quote with the full responsibilities and costs of employment.

Where can I check the underlying requirements?

  • Internal Revenue Service: Independent contractor (self-employed) or employee? — The classification test itself, which decides the real cost of a lean team.
  • U.S. Small Business Administration: Manage your business — Ongoing compliance obligations, which is the part an operating rhythm has to absorb.
