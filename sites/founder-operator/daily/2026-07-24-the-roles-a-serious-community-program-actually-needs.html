The Roles a Serious Community Program Actually Needs

A reliable community program needs clear ownership for strategy, programming, member experience, moderation, operations, analytics, and escalation. One person may cover several roles, but the responsibilities do not disappear just because the team is small.

Community staffing assigns responsibility for strategy, programming, member experience, moderation, operations, analytics and escalation. One person may cover several roles, but each responsibility still needs an owner and a backup arrangement. Define the work and its frequency before choosing job titles, headcount or an external operations partner.

What this guide is best for: The Roles a Serious Community Program Actually Needs

Topic context: community as a service.

What should I compare?

  • Name an executive sponsor and an accountable operator.
  • Assign programming and content production capacity.
  • Define moderation, safety, and escalation ownership.
  • Provide member support and onboarding coverage.
  • Reserve time for analytics, documentation, and improvement.

What questions should I ask?

  • Who makes strategic tradeoffs?
  • Who is present with members every day?
  • Who handles sensitive incidents?
  • Who owns event and content calendars?
  • Who turns activity data into decisions?

Which mistakes should I avoid?

Hiring a community manager with no cross-functional support. Leaving moderation rules implicit. Treating analytics as an end-of-quarter task. Depending on one person with no documentation or backup.
