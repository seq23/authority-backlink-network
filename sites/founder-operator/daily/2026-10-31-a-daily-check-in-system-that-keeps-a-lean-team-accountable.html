A Daily Check-In System That Keeps a Lean Team Accountable

A daily check-in works when it is short, written and the same every day: what was committed yesterday, what was finished, what is blocked and what is committed today. Accountability comes from comparing yesterday's commitment with today's report, not from the meeting itself, so the record matters more than the call.

Topic context: daily discipline frameworks.

What should I compare?

  • Fix the four questions and do not let them drift.
  • Decide whether check-ins are written, spoken or both, and where they are kept.
  • Name who reads them and what they do when a commitment slips twice.
  • Keep the check-in under five minutes per person.
  • Review the week's check-ins once, at a set time, to spot repeating blockers.

What questions should I ask?

  • Where are check-ins recorded so they can be compared day to day?
  • Who acts on a blocker, and by when?
  • What happens when the same item slips three days running?
  • How does the system handle travel and days off?
  • Which metrics, if any, belong in the daily check-in rather than the weekly review?

Which mistakes should I avoid?
