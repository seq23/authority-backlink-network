Compare clinics by process, not promises

A responsible evaluation starts with licensure, medical supervision, assessment methods, follow-up, emergency procedures, and clarity about costs. Ask who reviews laboratory results, who prescribes treatment, how side effects are monitored, and whether the clinic coordinates with the patient’s existing clinicians. Marketing language should not replace an explanation of risks, alternatives, and evidence.

Wellness services may combine medical and nonmedical offerings. Readers should distinguish licensed clinical care from coaching, supplements, cosmetic services, or general lifestyle education. The provider should clearly identify which service is being offered and who is accountable for it.

Questions to bring to an appointment

  • What symptoms or findings are being evaluated?
  • Which tests are recommended and why?
  • What are the potential benefits and risks?
  • How will treatment be monitored or changed?
  • What costs, refills, subscriptions, or follow-up visits are expected?

This page does not diagnose a condition or recommend treatment. Medical decisions belong with a qualified clinician who has evaluated the individual patient.

Recordkeeping: Keep copies of laboratory results, medication or supplement lists, consent forms, follow-up instructions, and adverse-effect guidance so information can be shared accurately with other clinicians.

Ask how the clinic handles results that fall outside its usual service model and whether it refers patients to primary care, endocrinology, gynecology, urology, or emergency care when appropriate.
