Separate an individual problem from a system problem

Burnout concerns may involve workload, role ambiguity, staffing, discrimination, caregiving, health, leadership behavior, or a mismatch between expectations and available resources. Personal coping practices can help, but they should not be used to hide an unsafe or unsustainable work design. Start by identifying the recurring conditions, their impact, and which changes are within the person’s control.

Document priorities, deadlines, workload changes, requests for support, and agreed responsibilities. A clear record can improve a conversation with a manager, human-resources professional, clinician, coach, or attorney, depending on the issue.

Boundary-setting questions

  • Which work is truly urgent?
  • What can be delayed, reassigned, or removed?
  • What communication window is reasonable?
  • Which accommodation, leave, or reporting process may apply?
  • Who can make the required organizational decision?

This page is educational and is not medical, mental-health, employment, or legal advice. Seek appropriate professional support when symptoms, safety, discrimination, retaliation, or legal rights are involved.

Safety note: When distress becomes severe, functioning changes abruptly, or there is risk of harm, use appropriate medical, mental-health, emergency, or crisis support rather than relying only on workplace planning tools.
