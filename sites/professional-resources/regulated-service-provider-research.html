Verify the provider and the scope

When a service is regulated, begin by confirming the provider’s identity, license or registration, location, and the specific work they are authorized to perform. Use the appropriate official regulator when possible rather than relying only on a badge displayed on a marketing page. Ask who will actually perform the work and who is responsible for supervision.

A written proposal should identify the service, exclusions, price, payment schedule, timeline, cancellation rules, and complaint process. If the provider handles health, legal, financial, immigration, safety, or other sensitive matters, clarify what information will be collected and how it will be protected.

Comparison questions

  • Is the provider currently authorized and in good standing?
  • What experience is relevant to this exact service?
  • What is included, excluded, or subcontracted?
  • What happens if the work is delayed or disputed?
  • Which records will the client receive?

A polished brand can support trust, but it is not a substitute for verification, written boundaries, and informed consent.

Before paying: Confirm the legal business name, payment recipient, refund terms, and milestone schedule. Avoid sending sensitive records or large payments through unverified channels.

If a provider refuses to identify the responsible licensee, provide written terms, or explain how complaints are handled, pause the transaction and verify the business before proceeding.
