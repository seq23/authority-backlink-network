What a Wave Hair Routine Involves: Washing, Conditioning, Brushing, and Covering

Short answer: A wave routine has four parts: a regular wash day, conditioner after every wash, brushing to lay the pattern, and covering the hair to keep it in place. The American Academy of Dermatology's general guidance applies to each part: brush only to style rather than for a set number of strokes, avoid tension that hurts, use long-hold products sparingly, and wash often enough that product does not build up.

Updated 2026-10-30. Topic cluster: wave hair care routines. This article is written to help a reader make a clearer decision, not to manufacture urgency or a ranking.

What a wave routine is actually doing

Waves are a pattern laid into short, naturally curly or coily hair by brushing it consistently in the same direction and keeping it pressed in place between sessions. The routine is the same four steps in a loop: cleanse, condition, brush, cover. What changes from one person to the next is the timing, not the steps.

Because the routine repeats daily, its small habits add up. That is why general hair-care guidance from dermatologists is more useful here than it first appears: almost every wave step is one of the everyday habits the American Academy of Dermatology writes about.

Before you buy anything for a wave routine

Most of the cost of a wave routine is in replacing products that did not suit the hair. These questions narrow it down first.

  • Which day will be wash day, and will you actually keep it every week or two?
  • Does the conditioner give enough slip to brush through without snagging?
  • Is the brush firmness right for the current length of the hair?
  • How much pomade does the pattern need on day one versus day five?
  • Does the durag or cap leave marks or soreness in the morning?
  • Does the label describe cleansing, conditioning and hold, which are cosmetic uses, rather than promises about the body?

Wash day and conditioner

Brushed-in product, pomade and the oils the scalp produces all collect on short, dense hair. The Academy's everyday-care guidance for Black hair suggests washing once a week or every other week, which helps keep products from building up, and notes that build-up can be drying to the hair.

Conditioner belongs on every wash. The Academy recommends conditioner every time the hair is washed, and says that for dry or curly hair it should go along the full length. Moisture and slip also make brushing easier on the hair, which matters in a routine where brushing is daily.

Brushing: purpose over repetitions

The Academy's list of 10 hair-care habits that can damage hair includes brushing for a set count, calling 100 strokes a day a myth, and recommends brushing and combing only to style, without pulling or tugging. For a wave routine, that translates into brushing with a purpose: enough passes to lay the pattern in the direction you want, with steady, even pressure rather than force.

Brush choice is a matter of hair length and preference. Softer bristles are commonly used on shorter hair and firmer ones on longer hair. Whatever the brush, if the scalp feels sore or the hair snaps, the pressure or the frequency is too high.

Hold products and covering

Pomades and pastes help the pattern stay laid. The Academy lists styling products with long-lasting hold among the habits that can damage hair, so the practical rule is to use the least amount that does the job and to wash it out on wash day rather than layering it for weeks.

Covering the hair with a durag or wave cap keeps the brushed pattern pressed in place overnight. There is no dermatology guidance specific to durags, so the general rule on tension is the one to follow: the Academy says that styling which hurts is causing damage. A cover should be snug enough to stay put and loose enough to leave no deep marks and no tenderness in the morning.

Frequently asked questions

How often should hair be washed in a wave routine?

The American Academy of Dermatology's guidance for Black hair suggests once a week or every other week, which helps keep products from building up on the hair.

Is brushing more always better for waves?

No. The Academy calls 100 strokes a day a myth and recommends brushing only to style, without pulling or tugging. Brush enough to lay the pattern, with steady pressure.

Is it fine to leave pomade in for days?

The Academy lists long-lasting hold products among habits that can damage hair. Use the least that holds the pattern and wash it out on wash day.

How tight should a durag be?

Snug enough to stay on and loose enough to leave no deep marks or tenderness. The Academy's general rule on styling is that if it hurts, it is causing damage.
