What to Ask About Confidentiality Before Using a Workplace Support Program

Short answer: Ask four questions before using an employer-funded support programme: who holds the records, what the employer receives and at what level of aggregation, what circumstances would cause information to be shared, and what happens to your file if you leave. The answers vary by programme and by jurisdiction, and they are reasonable to ask before the first session rather than after.

Updated 2026-08-31. Topic cluster: workplace wellbeing scoping. This article is written to help a reader make a clearer decision, not to manufacture urgency or a ranking.

What this page recommends

Ask four questions before using an employer-funded support programme: who holds the records, what the employer receives and at what level of aggregation, what circumstances would cause information to be shared, and what happens to your file if you leave.

The question is reasonable and rarely asked

People frequently avoid an employer-funded support programme because they are unsure what their employer will learn, and they often avoid asking as well, because asking feels like an admission. It is not. Providers answer this question routinely, and a provider who cannot answer it clearly has told you something useful.

It is also worth asking before the first session rather than during it. The answer may affect which route you choose, and it is much easier to consider calmly in advance.

Ask before the first session

Ask about the specific service you will use, not about the organization in general.

  • Which service this is, and whether it is delivered internally or externally.
  • Who holds the records and where they are stored.
  • What the employer receives, and at what level of aggregation.
  • Whether a small team size could make aggregate reporting identifying.
  • The circumstances in which information may be shared.
  • Whether any manager is told that you used the service.
  • What information travels with a referral, and whether you control it.
  • How long records are kept and what happens if you leave.
  • Whether the answers can be provided in writing.
  • Whether an independent route exists if you would rather the employer had no involvement.
  • Ask at least 2 days before a first session, so you have time to consider the answers.

What tends to differ between programmes

Who holds the records is the first difference. Some programmes are delivered by an external provider who holds the file; some are delivered internally. That distinction affects who can access what and under what circumstances.

What the employer receives is the second. Many programmes report usage in aggregate, such as how many sessions were used across the organization, without individual identification. Others report differently. Ask specifically what your employer sees, and whether it is possible for an individual to be identified in a small team.

The limits of confidentiality are the third, and every programme has them. Providers generally describe circumstances in which information may be shared, and these vary by profession and by jurisdiction. Ask what those circumstances are, in plain language, rather than assuming either that nothing is ever shared or that everything is.

The fourth is duration: how long records are kept, and what happens if you leave the organization. This is rarely mentioned and is straightforward to ask about.

Different services, different rules

A workplace programme, coaching, and clinical therapy are different services with different professional obligations, even when the same organization offers more than one. Do not assume the confidentiality arrangement is the same across them; ask about the specific service you are using.

If a referral is made from one to another, ask what information travels with the referral and whether you control that. Referrals are a common point at which people are surprised by what was passed on.

Finally, if the answer matters to a decision you are making, get it in writing. A written answer is not a guarantee, and it is a considerably better basis than a recollection of a phone call.

Frequently asked questions

Will my manager find out that I used the programme?

That depends on the programme's design and is exactly the question to ask before starting. Many programmes report only aggregate usage without individual identification, but arrangements differ, and small team sizes can make aggregate figures identifying. Ask the provider directly and ask for the answer in writing.

Are there always limits to confidentiality?

Yes. Every service has circumstances in which information may be shared, and those vary by profession, programme, and jurisdiction. A provider who says nothing is ever shared under any circumstances is oversimplifying. Ask for a plain-language description of what those circumstances are.

Is coaching covered by the same rules as therapy?

Generally not. Therapy is delivered by licensed clinicians under professional and legal obligations that coaching is not subject to in the same way. If the same organization offers both, ask about the specific service you are using rather than assuming the arrangements are identical.

Should I use an employer programme or find my own provider?

That is a personal decision and the answer often turns on the confidentiality arrangements you get. An employer programme may be quicker to access and may have limits you care about; an independent provider removes the employer from the arrangement but may cost more. Ask the questions first, then decide.
