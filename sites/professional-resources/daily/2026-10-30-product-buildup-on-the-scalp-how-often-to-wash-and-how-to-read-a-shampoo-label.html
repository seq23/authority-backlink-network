Product Buildup on the Scalp: How Often to Wash and How to Read a Shampoo Label

Short answer: Buildup is styling product, oil and dead skin collecting at the scalp between washes. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends shampooing the scalp rather than the full length, and for textured hair a wash once a week or every other week to keep product from building up. On labels, the FDA treats a cleansing shampoo as a cosmetic, and claims about changing the body make a product a drug.

Updated 2026-10-30. Topic cluster: cleansing and product buildup. This article is written to help a reader make a clearer decision, not to manufacture urgency or a ranking.

What buildup is and why it matters

Product buildup is what collects at the scalp between washes: styling products, oils, the scalp's own sebum and dead skin. The American Academy of Dermatology's guidance, Tips for healthy hair, describes shampoo's job in exactly those terms, as washing away built-up products, dead skin and excess oil.

Buildup is not only cosmetic. The Academy's everyday-care page for Black hair says that product build-up can be drying to the hair, and its general guidance lists not shampooing often enough among the common reasons for flakes. A scalp that feels coated, itchy by the end of the week, or flaky right before wash day is often telling you the interval is too long.

A wash-day checklist for buildup

One wash done in this order clears more than two done in a hurry.

  1. Detangle gently before wetting if the hair is long enough to knot.
  2. Wet the hair and scalp thoroughly before any product goes on.
  3. Work the shampoo or bar lather into the scalp with the fingertips, not the nails.
  4. Rinse until the water runs clear and the scalp no longer feels slick.
  5. Condition the ends, or the full length for dry or curly hair, and detangle with a wide-tooth comb while it is in.
  6. Rinse, blot with a towel or t-shirt, and apply only as much hold product as the style needs.
  7. Patch test any new product on a small area first, and stop using anything that stings or itches.

How often to wash to keep it off

The Academy's guidance ties washing frequency to how quickly the hair gets oily: someone with straight hair and an oily scalp may shampoo daily, while dry, textured, curly or thick hair can be washed when needed, at least once every 2 to 3 weeks. For Black hair specifically, it suggests once a week or every other week to keep product from building up.

Where the shampoo goes matters as much as how often. The Academy recommends applying it to the scalp instead of the entire length, so the scalp gets clean without the length being dried out. Conditioner then goes on after every wash.

Reading the label: cosmetic, drug, or soap

The FDA's page Is It a Cosmetic, a Drug, or Both? (Or Is It Soap?) explains that the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act defines cosmetics by their intended use, including cleansing and beautifying, and names cleansing shampoos as an example. A product whose intended use is to affect the structure or function of the body, or to address disease, is a drug. The FDA uses antidandruff shampoo as its example of a product that is both a cosmetic and a drug, and says such products must meet the requirements for both.

The same page explains that intended use can be established by the claims a seller makes, including on the internet. For a shopper, that gives a simple reading rule: words about cleansing, conditioning, softness and removing residue describe a cosmetic. Promises about what a product does to the body describe something else, and deserve a closer look.

Bars are a special case. The FDA explains that a product made mainly of alkali salts of fatty acids and sold only as soap falls under the Consumer Product Safety Commission rather than the FDA, but a bar of the same composition that is also sold for cosmetic uses, such as moisturizing, is regulated as a cosmetic. A shampoo and body bar sold for conditioning and cleansing hair sits on the cosmetic side of that line.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I have product buildup?

A coated or heavy feel before wash day, flakes near the end of the week, and dry-feeling hair despite an oily scalp are common signs. The American Academy of Dermatology lists infrequent shampooing among the common reasons for flakes.

How often should I wash to keep buildup off?

The Academy suggests washing dry, textured, curly or thick hair when needed and at least every 2 to 3 weeks, and once a week or every other week for Black hair to keep product from building up.

Should shampoo go on the full length of the hair?

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends applying shampoo to the scalp instead of the entire length, which clears buildup where it collects without drying the rest of the hair.

Is a shampoo bar a soap or a cosmetic?

The FDA explains that a bar sold only as soap falls under the Consumer Product Safety Commission, while one also sold for cosmetic uses such as moisturizing or conditioning is regulated as a cosmetic.
