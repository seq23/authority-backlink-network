What Levels of Support Mean When Comparing Recovery Residences

Short answer: The National Alliance for Recovery Residences, known as NARR, publishes a national standard that groups recovery residences into four levels of support, ranging from peer-run homes with no on-site staff through to residences with clinical oversight. What the framework is for

Recovery residences are described with a confusing variety of terms, and the same words mean different things in different places. The National Alliance for Recovery Residences publishes a national standard that gives the field a shared vocabulary by grouping residences into four levels of support.

The levels describe how a residence is staffed and structured, from democratically run peer homes with no staff on site through to settings with clinical oversight. They are a description of type, not a ranking of quality: a well-run residence at one level may suit a person far better than a poorly run one at another.

Verifying what a residence tells you

Ask these of every residence you are comparing, and write the answers down.

  • Which level of support the residence describes itself as, and why.
  • Whether staff are on site, and during which hours.
  • Which organization certifies it, and whether that certification is current.
  • Whether your state has its own registry or requirements in addition.
  • What clinical services, if any, are provided on site rather than referred.
  • How decisions about the house are made, and by whom.
  • The expected length of stay and what happens at the end of it.
  • What happens if a resident needs more support than the house provides.
  • Whether you can speak with a current or former resident.
  • Whether you can visit before committing.
  • The answers in writing, with at least 2 days to compare them before you decide.

How to use it when comparing houses

The practical value is that it converts a vague question, is this house more structured than that one, into a specific one. Asking a residence which level it describes itself as, and why, produces a much clearer answer than asking how much support it offers.

It also gives families a way to check that expectations match. A person leaving a structured programme may need more staffing than a peer-run house provides, and that mismatch is easier to spot with shared terminology than without it.

What the framework does not tell you is whether a specific house is well run, how residents are treated, or whether it will suit an individual. Those questions still need the resident agreement, a visit if possible, and conversations with people who have lived there.

Certification and what to verify locally

Certification against the standard is administered through state affiliate organizations, which means the certifying body, the process, and sometimes the terminology differ depending on where you are. Some states also operate their own registries or requirements that sit alongside it.

So the useful question to a residence is specific rather than general: are you certified, by which organization, and is that certification current. Ask to see it, and ask the affiliate directly if you want to confirm.

Certification is a floor rather than a guarantee. It indicates that a residence has been assessed against a published standard; it does not describe day-to-day life in the house, which is what the resident agreement and current residents can tell you.

Frequently asked questions

What is NARR?

The National Alliance for Recovery Residences is a national organization that publishes a standard for recovery residences and works through state affiliate organizations. Its standard defines four levels of support that describe how residences are staffed and structured, which gives the field shared terminology it otherwise lacks.

Is a higher level better?

No. The levels describe staffing and structure, not quality. A peer-run house with no staff on site is the right setting for some people and the wrong one for others. The useful question is which level matches the person's current needs, not which number is highest.

Does certification mean a residence is good?

It means an external body has assessed it against a published standard, which is meaningful and is not the same as a judgment about daily life in the house. Treat it as a floor, then read the resident agreement and, where possible, speak with people who have lived there.

How do I check a certification?

Ask the residence which organization certified it and when, then contact that state affiliate directly to confirm it is current. Because certification is administered locally, the process and the certifying body differ by state, so the affiliate is the right place to ask rather than a national listing.
