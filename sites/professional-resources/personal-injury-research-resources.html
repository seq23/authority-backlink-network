Preserve information after an accident

People often need to make decisions while dealing with medical care, repairs, insurance calls, and missed work. Keep a dated record of what happened, photographs, contact information, medical documents, bills, insurance correspondence, and receipts. Do not alter evidence or rely on memory when a contemporaneous note can be made.

Deadlines and legal rights vary by jurisdiction and facts. General online material cannot determine liability, value, or whether a claim should be filed. Be cautious about signing releases, giving broad authorizations, or making recorded statements without understanding their purpose and consequences.

Questions for a qualified attorney

  • What deadlines may apply?
  • What evidence should be preserved?
  • How are fees and case expenses handled?
  • Who will manage communications and updates?
  • What outcomes and risks are realistic?

This page is educational and does not create an attorney-client relationship. A licensed attorney must evaluate the specific facts and applicable law.

Practical caution: Continue appropriate medical care and follow professional instructions. Legal research should not be used to delay emergency treatment or to make clinical decisions without a qualified provider.
