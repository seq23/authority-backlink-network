Memphis Party Decor Vendor Questions

A Memphis party-decor vendor brief is a record of the occasion, venue permissions, materials and installation work requested from a styling provider. It allows providers to quote against shared assumptions. Photographic inspiration describes a desired look; the brief must also cover access, removal and what the booking actually includes.

Quick answer

Confirm venue permissions and access first, then ask each provider to itemize installation, materials and removal against the same brief.

What this guide is best for: What should I send a party-decor provider.

What should I decide first?

  1. Send the venue, date, installation window, guest flow, photographs and measurements that show the usable space.
  2. Separate design, materials, delivery, installation, collection and rentals in the offers you receive.
  3. Ask about load-in, permitted attachments, power, exits, guest circulation and removal deadlines.

Frequently asked questions

What should I send a party-decor provider?

Send the venue, date, installation window, guest flow, photographs and measurements that show the usable space. Describe the occasion and desired focal points without assuming a reference photograph will fit. Ask the provider what it still needs before quoting and who must approve attachments or outside-vendor access.

How do I avoid comparing different scopes?

Separate design, materials, delivery, installation, collection and rentals in the offers you receive. Ask each provider to identify exclusions and changes that could affect the quote. The vendor-quote guide covers this comparison in detail; this page is the starting brief for locating and questioning a suitable provider.

What should the venue confirm?

Ask about load-in, permitted attachments, power, exits, guest circulation and removal deadlines. For a hotel, ask how room access is authorized before promising a surprise installation. Local authorities publish relevant requirements, but the venue and provider must confirm how those apply to the proposed materials and setup.

Define the decorating scope

Party décor can mean a few delivered pieces or a complete installation with balloons, florals, backdrops, signage, linens, lighting, and teardown. Before comparing vendors, list the spaces to be decorated, the event schedule, the venue’s access rules, and which items must be removed the same day. Inspiration photos are useful, but they do not replace measurements or a written inventory.

Ask whether the proposal includes design work, delivery, installation, rentals, labor, taxes, damage deposits, and pickup. Confirm who is responsible if the venue changes the setup window or rejects an installation method.

Venue and safety checks

  • Confirm ceiling, wall, and floor attachment rules.
  • Ask about flame, electrical, and aisle restrictions.
  • Verify setup and teardown access.
  • Identify weather alternatives for outdoor décor.
  • Assign one person to approve final placement.

For weddings, coordinate décor timing with photography, catering, entertainment, and venue turnover. The decorator should know when the room must be complete and when other vendors need uninterrupted access.

Final confirmation: Ask for a simple installation sketch or item list and confirm the final room layout before delivery. This reduces last-minute conflicts with catering, entertainment, photography, and guest circulation.

Keep the final proposal, payment receipt, installation window, and teardown plan in the same event file. That gives the venue and planner a dependable record if staff or timing changes.
