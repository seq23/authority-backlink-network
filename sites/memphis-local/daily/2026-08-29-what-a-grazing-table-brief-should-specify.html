What a Grazing Table Brief Should Specify

Short answer: A grazing table brief needs eight specifics: guest count, how long the table is on service, whether it is the meal or an accompaniment, dietary requirements, the table or surface available, access and setup time, who clears it, and whether refrigeration is available on site. Miss any of them and the quote you receive is a guess.

Guest count is not the same as portions

The first number a provider needs is guest count, but the number that actually drives the quote is what the table is expected to do. A grazing table that replaces a meal is a different volume of food from one that accompanies drinks before a sit-down dinner.

Say which it is, plainly. Providers hear "a grazing table for sixty" and have to guess, and the two possible interpretations differ substantially in both quantity and cost.

Questions to ask the provider before booking

These are the answers that separate providers who do this regularly from those who do not.

  • How do you handle replenishment across a long service window?
  • What is your procedure for holding food safely, and who monitors it during the event?
  • How do you handle allergies, and how are those items kept separate?
  • How long do you need on site to set up, and what access do you require?
  • Do you supply the table, or are you working to ours?
  • What does the table look like at the end, and do you restyle it partway through?
  • Who clears the table, and is waste removal included?
  • What happens if the guest count changes, and by when do you need to know?

The service window is the constraint people forget

How long the table stays out is a real constraint rather than a preference, and it interacts with food safety, with the venue, and with how the table looks at the end. A spread that is beautiful at the start of a long window can look picked over well before the event ends.

Ask the provider how they handle a long window: whether they replenish, whether some items are held back and added later, and whether anything gets removed at a set time. A provider with a clear answer has done this before.

Time and temperature requirements for held food are set by food-safety authorities and by the provider's own procedures, not by the host. Ask the provider what their procedure is and who is responsible for monitoring it during the event.

The physical setup

Grazing tables need a surface, and the surface is frequently assumed. Confirm what table is available, its dimensions, whether the provider brings one, and whether the venue permits their own. A design built for eight feet does not compress gracefully onto six.

Setup takes longer than people expect, and it is not a job that can be rushed at the last minute without the result showing. Ask how long the provider needs, and check that the venue's access window actually allows it.

Finally, agree who clears. Grazing tables leave a substantial amount of material behind, and at the end of an event nobody wants to discover that clearing it was assumed to be somebody else's job.

Frequently asked questions

How much food does a grazing table need per guest?

Ask the provider rather than working from a general figure, because the honest answer depends on whether the table is the meal, what else is being served, the length of the event, and the audience. A provider who quotes a quantity should be able to explain which of those assumptions they used.

How long can a grazing table stay out?

That is a food-safety question governed by your local health authority's requirements and by the provider's own procedures, not something a general guide should put a number on. Ask the provider directly what their limit is, how they monitor it, and what happens to items at the end of the window.

Can a grazing table accommodate allergies?

Providers vary in what they can safely offer, and the honest answers range from full separation to none. Tell the provider the specific allergies and how many guests are affected, and ask how items are prepared, kept apart, and labelled. Guests with serious allergies should be told what is and is not possible.

Do grazing tables work outdoors?

They can, with more planning: shade, wind, insects, and holding temperature all become harder outdoors. Raise the outdoor plan early, ask what changes in the design, and agree a contingency in case the weather moves the event indoors.
