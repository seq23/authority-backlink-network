What to Check About Your Porch Before Booking a Decorating Service

Short answer: Check five things before you book: the measurements, the power situation, what the surfaces will tolerate being attached to, how a crew gets in, and whether any HOA or lease rule applies. Most quote revisions and install-day surprises trace back to one of these five being discovered after the design was agreed.

The survey takes 20 minutes and saves a revision

Providers quote from what you tell them. When the description is a photograph and a vibe, the quote is an estimate against assumptions, and at least one of those assumptions is usually wrong. Twenty minutes with a tape measure and a phone converts an estimate into something firmer, and the 8 items below are all of it.

It also changes the conversation. A provider who receives measurements, an outlet location, and a note about the HOA rule on attachments is dealing with someone who has done the groundwork, and the design that comes back is more likely to be buildable.

Photograph these before you request a quote

Take the photos in daylight. Include something for scale in at least one of them.

  • The full frontage from the street, so the provider sees proportion.
  • The porch from the doorway looking out.
  • A close view of each column, post, or railing that might be wrapped.
  • The front door itself, including any glass, hardware, or existing wreath hook.
  • The outlet you intend to use, close enough to see whether it is weather-rated.
  • The approach from the drive or street, showing steps and width.
  • Any surface you are unsure about attaching to.
  • Anything already on the porch that has to stay, such as furniture or planters.

Surfaces and what may be attached to them

The most common install-day problem is an attachment method the surface will not tolerate. Painted wood, brick, vinyl siding, stone, and metal railings all behave differently, and some of them keep a permanent record of whatever was attached to them.

If you rent, this is not only a preference question. Leases frequently restrict what may be fixed to exterior surfaces, and the deposit consequence lands on you rather than on the provider. Read the clause before the design is finalised.

If a piece is heavy or a structure is being freestanding, that becomes a load question rather than a decor question. Anything that concerns you structurally should go to a qualified contractor, not to a decorator.

Access, power, and rules

Crews arrive with a vehicle and materials. Narrow drives, gated entries, steep steps, and street parking restrictions all change how long the job takes. Say what applies rather than letting it be discovered.

Power is the detail most often assumed. Confirm which outlet is available, whether it is a weather-rated exterior outlet, whether it is on an indoor switch, and whether anything else is already drawing from it. A display designed around power that does not exist gets rebuilt on the day.

Finally, check the rules. Homeowner associations, historic district guidelines, and rental agreements can all restrict exterior decor in ways that are easy to check in advance and expensive to discover afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need exact measurements?

Approximate is usually enough for a first quote, but exact figures for column height and railing length are worth the extra minutes, because those two drive material quantities. A design priced against a guessed column height is the most common reason a quote gets revised after the site visit.

What if I rent rather than own?

Check the lease for clauses about exterior alterations and attachments before the design is agreed, and tell the provider what is permitted. Providers can usually work with non-marking methods when they know the constraint in advance, and cannot retrofit that constraint once the design assumes otherwise.

Does the provider do a site visit?

Some do and some quote from photographs, and the answer often depends on the size of the job. Ask directly, and ask whether a site visit changes the quote. Sending thorough photographs and measurements makes a remote quote considerably more reliable either way.

What if my HOA has rules about decorations?

Read them before booking and share the relevant part with the provider. Associations commonly restrict size, duration, attachment to shared surfaces, and lighting. This publication does not interpret covenants; take a genuinely unclear clause to your association or to qualified counsel rather than guessing.
