The Minimum Information to Gather Before Comparing Wedding Vendors

Vendor comparison becomes more useful after the couple defines date, location, guest range, budget range, service scope, style priorities, decision makers, and non-negotiable constraints. Without that foundation, quotes are difficult to compare and conversations repeat.

A wedding vendor brief records the date, location, guest range, budget boundaries, service scope and style priorities before quotes are requested. It also names decision makers and practical constraints. Giving vendors the same information makes proposals easier to compare and reduces repeated conversations about missing details.

What this guide is best for: The Minimum Information to Gather Before Comparing Wedding Vendors

Topic context: wedding planning gaps.

What should I compare?

  • Create one shared planning brief.
  • Separate must-haves from preferences.
  • Use the same scope when requesting quotes.
  • Record exclusions and assumptions, not just price.
  • Keep questions and decisions attached to each vendor category.

What questions should I ask?

  • What exact service is being priced?
  • What is excluded?
  • Which dates or counts can still change?
  • Who makes the final decision?
  • What would make the option unacceptable?

Which mistakes should I avoid?

Requesting quotes with different scopes. Comparing headline price without exclusions. Changing guest count during vendor evaluation. Failing to record why an option was rejected.
