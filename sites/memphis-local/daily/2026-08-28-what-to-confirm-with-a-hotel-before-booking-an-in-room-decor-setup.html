What to Confirm With a Hotel Before Booking an In-Room Decor Setup

Short answer: Confirm four things with the hotel in writing before you book anyone: whether outside vendors are permitted, how and when they get access to the room, what is prohibited inside the room, and who is responsible for cleanup and any damage charge. A decor booking that assumes access is the most common way these setups fail.

Access is the whole problem

A room setup happens in a space you do not control, at a time you may not control, in a building with its own rules. Everything else about the booking is straightforward by comparison. The question that decides whether it works is who lets the decorator into the room and when.

There are usually three workable patterns: the guest checks in and lets the vendor in personally, hotel staff escort the vendor, or the vendor is on an approved list and is granted access directly. Properties differ, and the same property can differ by shift. Get the answer from someone who can commit to it, and get it in writing.

The email to send the hotel

One message, sent well ahead of the date, covering these eight points. Keep the reply.

  1. The reservation name, confirmation number, and date.
  2. That an outside decor vendor will be attending, with the company name.
  3. The requested access window, and who will let them in.
  4. A short description of what will be set up.
  5. A direct question about candles, attachments, petals, confetti, and balloons.
  6. Confirmation of who removes the setup and by when.
  7. Any cleaning or damage fee the property would apply.
  8. A request for a named on-site contact and a direct number for the evening.

What hotels commonly restrict

Open flame is the most common restriction, and it usually covers real candles regardless of how they are contained. Battery alternatives are the standard workaround and are worth asking about specifically rather than assuming.

Attachment to walls, doors, and fixtures is the second. Adhesive that lifts paint or leaves residue can produce a damage charge that lands on whoever booked the room. Ask what is permitted and tell the vendor before they design.

Some properties restrict loose materials such as glitter, confetti, and flower petals, either outright or with a cleaning fee attached. That fee is often larger than people expect, and it is charged after the fact, which is a bad time to discover it.

Balloons are usually fine but can be restricted in properties with high ceilings or sensitive smoke detectors. It costs nothing to ask.

Getting it in writing

Verbal permission from whoever answers the phone is not enough, because the person on duty when the vendor arrives may not know about it. Email is the useful medium: a short message confirming vendor access, the time window, and what is permitted, with a reply you can show at the desk.

Include the vendor's name and arrival time in that email. Front desks handle these requests routinely when they are expected and awkwardly when they are not.

Finally, ask who to contact if something goes wrong at eight in the evening. A named contact and a direct number is worth more than any other detail in the arrangement.

Frequently asked questions

Will a hotel let a decorator into the room before check-in?

Some will and some will not, and it often depends on housekeeping schedules on the day rather than on policy. Ask specifically rather than assuming, and have a fallback plan where the guest lets the vendor in after check-in, since that path is available at almost every property.

Are real candles ever allowed?

Usually not in guest rooms, and the restriction typically applies regardless of containment. Battery-operated alternatives are the common substitute and are worth confirming as acceptable in the same message, since a small number of properties restrict anything that resembles a flame.

Who pays if the property charges a cleaning fee?

Whoever holds the reservation, in almost every case, which is why the question belongs in the confirmation email rather than in the vendor contract. If a fee applies to petals or confetti, decide before booking whether the look is worth it.

How far ahead should this be arranged?

Start the conversation with the property at least 2 weeks out where possible. Vendor approval, access arrangements, and any required paperwork take longer at larger properties, and a short-notice request is more likely to be refused simply because there is no time to process it.
