Memphis Wedding Cost by Guest Count, 2026 Edition

Nobody publishes rigorous Memphis-specific wedding pricing, so this publication collected it. 21 Memphis-area vendors publish 70 prices on their own websites; this page records all of them, derives what they imply about cost by guest count, and states plainly what the sample does not cover.

What this page recommends

Budget the fixed vendors first and the per-guest vendors second: in this sample the fixed block does not move with guest count at all, so the cost per guest falls steeply as the list grows, and cutting the guest list saves far less than people expect.

Method, sample and limits

Sample: 21 Memphis-area wedding and event vendors, 70 individual published prices.

Collection window: 2026-09-01 to 2026-09-01. Every price was read from the vendor's own website on that date.

Method: Every candidate vendor's own website was fetched over HTTP and read as text. A vendor entered the dataset only if a numeric price appeared on a page the vendor controls. Marketplace listings (The Knot, WeddingWire, Zola, Thumbtack, Peerspace, Wedding Spot, Eventective) were used to find candidates and were never used as a price source, because the price shown there is a range the platform assembles rather than the vendor's published rate.

Coverage: Memphis, Tennessee metropolitan area, including Germantown and Bartlett, TN and the north Mississippi tri-state area vendors serve from Memphis.

What this is not: not a survey, not an average of what couples paid, and not a ranking. It is a record of what these vendors publish.

What this data does not cover

Stated before the numbers rather than after them, because the gaps decide what the numbers can honestly be used for.

  • Alcohol and bar packages. No vendor in this sample publishes a per-person bar price, so the guest-count model below contains no alcohol line at all. This is the single largest gap.
  • Videography, attire, rings, stationery, hair and makeup, transport, hotel blocks and honeymoon. None were collected.
  • Actual contracted prices. Everything here is a published list or starting price. What couples pay after customisation, negotiation, peak-date premiums and overtime is not observable from a website and is not claimed.
  • Tennessee state and local sales tax, service charges and gratuity, except where a vendor publishes them as a line item.
  • Any claim of a market average. Several categories have one publishing vendor. A category's sample size is printed next to it, and a sample of one is labelled as a sample of one rather than as an average.
  • Vendors who do not publish prices, which is most of them. That is a selection bias in a knowable direction: vendors who post a starting price are more likely to be competing on transparency or on price, so this sample probably sits below the market it is drawn from.

Cost by guest count, from published Memphis prices

Guest count is the largest single cost driver in this category, and it is the thing national wedding sites answer worst: they publish a national average and apply a city multiplier, which is not a measurement of Memphis. The shape is the finding. The all-in figure per guest falls from $216.26–$580.10 at 50 guests to $71.26–$175.55 at 200, because most of what these vendors publish is a fixed cost being divided across more people. Read the other way: trimming the guest list saves only the per-guest column, which is the smaller half of the bill at every count in this table.

These totals are lower than the national per-guest figures for a reason stated above and worth repeating here: this dataset contains no alcohol, attire, rings, stationery, videography, transport or beauty costs, because no vendor in the sample publishes a price for them. It is a narrower measurement, not a cheaper city.

How to check or reproduce this

  1. Open any source link in the table. The figure quoted here should appear on that page.
  2. If it has changed, the price moved after the observation date shown in the row. That is expected, and it is why the date is published next to every figure.
  3. To rebuild the guest-count table, take the per-guest inputs, multiply by the guest count, and add the fixed block. There is no weighting, no smoothing and no adjustment factor.
  4. If you find an error, the corrections address is on the corrections page and a correction will be logged there rather than quietly edited.

Questions about this dataset

How many vendors is this based on?

21 Memphis-area vendors, and 70 individual published prices, all read from the vendors' own websites on 2026-09-01. Some categories are a sample of one. Where that is true it is labelled on the table rather than presented as an average.

Why is the per-guest figure lower than the national numbers?

Because this dataset covers less. National per-guest figures of roughly $235 to $400 include alcohol, attire, rings, stationery, videography, transport and hair and makeup. None of those are in this sample, because no vendor in it publishes a price for them. The figures here are not a smaller version of the national number; they are a different, narrower measurement.

Why does the cost per guest fall as the guest count rises?

Two published effects, both visible in the table. Most of what Memphis vendors publish is fixed: a photographer, a DJ, a coordinator and a venue cost the same for 50 guests as for 200, so the fixed block is divided across more people. On top of that, the one caterer in this sample that publishes banded pricing charges less per person at higher counts.

Can I use these numbers?

Yes. The table and the CSV are published so they can be quoted, and every figure names the vendor page it came from and the date it was read. Please cite the source URL for the figure rather than this page alone, and note that published prices change.

Why are most Memphis vendors missing?

Because most do not publish a price. A vendor that answers pricing only by email cannot be in a desk-researched dataset without inventing something, so it is not here. That is a selection bias with a knowable direction: vendors who post a starting price are more likely to be competing on transparency or price, so this sample probably sits below the market it is drawn from.
