Grazing tables and Memphis event vendors

A grazing table is a shared arrangement of food that guests serve themselves from over a planned service period. Planning it requires decisions about its role in the meal, dietary requirements, replenishment and food handling. Compare it with buffet or passed service by staffing, access and the way guests will be served.

Quick answer

Brief the provider on guest count, service duration, meal role and venue conditions; verify food-handling arrangements with the relevant local authority.

What this guide is best for: Briefing a grazing table, comparing it against a buffet, and finding and questioning local vendors.

Frequently asked questions

What belongs in a grazing-table brief?

State whether the table is a meal or an accompaniment, who will attend, the service period and where it will sit. Ask how dietary needs are handled and who replenishes or clears it. A visual reference is useful for presentation, but it does not explain the serving plan or the venue’s restrictions.

How does grazing differ from a buffet?

Compare the flow of guests and the responsibilities of staff rather than choosing from photographs alone. A shared display, a plated queue and passed food place different demands on circulation and replenishment. Ask the provider how its proposal works in your room and what happens when guests arrive together.

Who confirms food-service requirements in Memphis?

Shelby County Health Department is the local source to consult about applicable food-service requirements. The FDA Food Code is a model code, not proof of a particular vendor’s permit or compliance. Ask for the provider’s relevant documentation and handling plan; this publication does not certify food safety or inspect vendors.

Where can I check the underlying requirements?

  • Shelby County Health Department: Shelby County Health Dept., TN — The county authority that actually permits and inspects food service in Memphis.
  • U.S. Food and Drug Administration: FDA Food Code — The model code state and county food rules are written from, including time and temperature control for safety.
