Wedding planning tools and checklists

A wedding planning tool is a record of decisions, responsibilities and deadlines for an event. A checklist tracks what remains to be settled, while a timeline, seating chart and budget handle different constraints. Choose a tool by the decision it helps make rather than the amount of detail it contains.

Quick answer

Anchor the checklist to your date and confirmed bookings; record an owner and the evidence needed to finish each task.

What this guide is best for: Date-based checklists, planning templates and the gaps that show up between booking and the week of the wedding.

Frequently asked questions

Which wedding planning tool should I start with?

Start with the unresolved decision that blocks other work. A date and venue constraint may come before a seating chart; a budget record may expose commitments before another booking. Keep the checklist as an index of those decisions, with links to the detailed records instead of copying every figure into every tool.

How do I prevent gaps between vendors?

Assign an owner to each handoff, including room access, deliveries, setup and collection. Write down who confirms that the receiving person is ready. A vendor completing its own scope does not necessarily complete the next vendor’s prerequisites; the planning record should make that dependency visible before the event week.

What should be checked before a plan is shared?

Check the event date, version, contact roles and source of each deadline. Separate a vendor’s confirmed contractual requirement from a suggested planning date. Remove private guest details from broadly shared copies. The version sent to a vendor should show the actions that vendor needs, without exposing unrelated family information.
