Wedding day timelines

A wedding-day timeline is a sequence of event cues, access windows and vendor handoffs for the wedding day. It translates a guest-facing schedule into practical responsibilities. A usable timeline records who starts each action, what must already be ready and who can approve a change when timing slips.

Quick answer

Build backwards from fixed venue and ceremony constraints, then confirm setup, travel and handoff assumptions with the people responsible for them.

What this guide is best for: Building a run-of-day timeline vendors can work from, and what changes when the schedule slips.

Frequently asked questions

Where should I begin a wedding-day timeline?

Identify the commitments that cannot move freely: venue access, ceremony arrangements, service windows and the end of the booking. Work back through preparation and transport from those anchors. Ask each vendor to confirm its own duration rather than borrowing a generic schedule that assumes different locations or crew responsibilities.

How do I show buffers without hiding them?

Name the purpose of each buffer, such as travel uncertainty or a room change, instead of placing unexplained gaps between events. Keep private preparation details separate from the version guests receive. A coordinator needs to know which time is a target and which is a contractual or venue constraint.

Who should change the schedule during the day?

Choose a person authorized to coordinate timing changes and record how affected vendors will hear about them. Keep a current version accessible to that person. When a cue slips, review the dependencies before moving the next item; a later food service or venue access window may not move with it.
