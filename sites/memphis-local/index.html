Plan the event before comparing vendors

Vendor selection becomes easier when the event has a defined purpose, guest range, location, budget, and decision owner. A wedding may also require a checklist, payment schedule, day-of timeline, seating plan, meal tracking, and clear handoffs among the venue and vendors. A smaller party may need fewer documents, but the same basic discipline prevents missed expectations.

Use the local guides to identify categories and questions. Use planning tools only when they solve the problem described in the article. A decorating resource should not be forced into a budgeting page, and a wedding tool should not appear in an unrelated local vendor article.

Local-first, not local-only

Memphis-specific pages may discuss local venues, seasonal conditions, neighborhoods, transport, or setup constraints. The library may also publish universal guidance such as organizing deposits, building a timeline, or preparing table assignments. Those pages remain appropriate because they serve Memphis readers even when the method works elsewhere.

Before hiring, ask for a written scope, payment schedule, cancellation terms, access requirements, and the name of the person responsible on event day. Confirm what is included, what costs extra, and who handles cleanup or last-minute changes.

Planning tip: Keep one shared event record with the guest count, budget, contracts, payment dates, contacts, access times, and final decisions. That single source reduces contradictory instructions across vendors.

When several people are planning together, name one final decision owner and one vendor-contact owner. Clear ownership prevents duplicate instructions, missed approvals, and last-minute changes that no one has authority to resolve.
